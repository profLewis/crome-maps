USDA Cropland Data Layer (CDL)

Summary

Annual 30m crop type classification for the contiguous United States, produced by the USDA National Agricultural Statistics Service (NASS). The CDL is the reference crop map for the US, covering 130+ crop types from 2008 to present. Derived from Landsat and other satellite imagery using decision tree classification trained on Farm Service Agency (FSA) Common Land Unit data.

CoverageContiguous United States
Resolution30m (Landsat)

Description

The Cropland Data Layer is an annual raster land cover classification focused on agricultural land in the US. It uses satellite imagery (primarily Landsat, supplemented by Sentinel-2 and other sensors) classified with a decision tree algorithm trained on ground truth from USDA Farm Service Agency records. The CDL identifies 130+ land cover types with emphasis on specific crop types and double-cropping practices.

Provenance

Produced by USDA NASS Research and Development Division. Training data from FSA Common Land Units (CLUs) — field-level administrative records similar to EU LPIS. Classification: decision tree on multi-temporal Landsat + ancillary data (elevation, soil, climate). Post-processing: crop sequence filtering using prior year CDL.

Classification System

130+ classes including major crops (corn, soybeans, wheat, cotton, rice, sorghum), minor crops (dry beans, lentils, canola), double crops (winter wheat/soybeans), and non-crop (forest, water, developed, wetlands). Uses NASS crop coding scheme.

Accuracy & Validation

Accuracy varies significantly by crop, state, and year. In major producing states (Corn Belt), corn and soybeans achieve >90% accuracy. Minor crops and crops in non-core regions can fall below 70%. Statewide overall accuracy (FSA crop categories) ranges from ~70% (Texas, diverse crops/small fields) to ~90% (Iowa/Illinois, dominated by corn/soy). Validated annually using independent FSA Common Land Unit data withheld from training.

State-level overall accuracy (2023 CDL):
Iowa: 90.5% (κ=0.83) · Illinois: 90.2% (κ=0.83) · Arkansas: 89.3% (κ=0.85) · Kansas: 81.5% (κ=0.78) · California: 80.7% (κ=0.79) · North Dakota: 77.5% (κ=0.74) · Texas: 70.0% (κ=0.64).

Key patterns: Accuracy is highest in the Corn Belt where corn and soy dominate; lowest in diverse-crop states (Texas, California). Winter wheat is consistently the hardest major crop to classify (37–89% producer's accuracy) due to spectral similarity with grassland. Rice achieves near-perfect accuracy in California (98/99%).

State-level accuracy reports for all 50 states (1997–2023) available at NASS CDL Metadata. Individual state pages: metadata_{state}{yy}.htm (e.g., Iowa 2023, Kansas 2023).
